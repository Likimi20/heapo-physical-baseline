### 1. Prebound Effect: Definition and Causes
* **Definition**: The prebound effect is defined as the gap or deviation between calculated energy demand and actual measured space heating consumption in existing buildings.
  * **Source Title**: *TABULA Common Calculation Method* (2013), Section 6, p. 27; *The prebound-effect in detail: real indoor temperatures in basements and measured versus calculated U-values* (2017), Section 1, p. 32–33.
  * **Building Type**: Existing residential dwellings (single-family and multi-family).
  * **Data Year**: 2012 / 2013 / 2017.
* **Primary Causes Identified in the Sources**:
  1. Calculated U-values in building audits/regulations are systematically higher (worse) than actual measured U-values.
  2. Assumed unheated basement/cellar temperatures in normative standards are significantly lower than real measured temperatures.
  3. Discrepancies exist between actual local outdoor temperatures and official climate datasets.
  4. Real winter indoor room temperatures in unrenovated old buildings are typically lower than standard nominal assumptions due to night setbacks, unheated buffer rooms, and higher costs per degree of temperature increase.
  * **Source Title**: *The prebound-effect in detail: real indoor temperatures in basements and measured versus calculated U-values* (2017), Section 1, p. 32–33; *TABULA Common Calculation Method* (2013), Section 6, p. 27.

---

### 2. Measured versus Calculated U-Values in Existing Buildings
* **Source Title**: *The prebound-effect in detail: real indoor temperatures in basements and measured versus calculated U-values* (Caroline Hoffmann & Achim Geissler, FHNW / Energy Procedia 122)
* **Data Year**: 2017 (measurements conducted in winter 2015/16, Jan–Feb 2016)
* **Overall Component Deviation**: For 7 out of 9 uninsulated/existing building wall constructions tested, calculated U-values (determined independently by 10 GEAK energy consultants using SN EN ISO 6946) were higher (worse) than measured values (using heat flux sensors), with measured values being **12% to 111% lower (better)** than calculated consultant averages (Section 3.1 & Section 4.1, p. 34–35).
* **Specific Building Element Comparisons** (Table 1 & Figure 1a, p. 33–34):
  * **Zürich 01** (*Office / Non-residential*, built 1951, wall built 1905, 30 cm brick):
    * Measured U-value: **1.2 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **1.5 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Münchenstein 01 B1** (*Single-family house*, built 1925, basement wall, 40 cm tamped concrete):
    * Measured U-value: **1.8 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **2.1 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Münchenstein 01 G** (*Single-family house*, built 1925, ground floor wall, 2×12 cm brick with 6 cm air gap):
    * Measured U-value: **1.1 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **1.2 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Rheinfelden 01** (*Building type not specified / historical*, built ~1600, 55 cm rubble sandstone masonry):
    * Measured U-value: **0.6 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **1.3 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Küttingen 01** (*Building type not specified*, built 1953, 30 cm reinforced concrete):
    * Measured U-value: **2.7 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **3.0 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Wegenstetten 01** (*Building type not specified*, built 1850, 67.5 cm limestone):
    * Measured U-value: **1.1 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **1.0 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Muhen 01** (*Building type not specified*, built 1965, 2×12 cm honeycomb brick with 3 cm glass wool):
    * Measured U-value: **0.6 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **0.7 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Reinach 01 IC** (*Multi-family house*, built 1962/63, 6 cm mineral wool + 30 cm concrete):
    * Measured U-value: **0.7 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **0.5 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
  * **Reinach 01 USC** (*Multi-family house*, built 1962/63, 30 cm uninsulated special concrete):
    * Measured U-value: **1.3 W/(m²K)** | Calculated Mean: **2.7 W/(m²K)** (Data year: 2016)
* **Consultant Calculation Variance**: The standard deviation of calculated values reported by the 10 energy consultants ranged between **0.04 W/(m²K)** (Reinach 01 IC) and **0.34 W/(m²K)** (Reinach 01 USC) (Figure 1a caption, p. 34).
* **Physical Cause & Demand Impact**: Normative U-value calculation (SN EN ISO 6946) assumes a conservative external wind velocity of **4 m/s** (\\(h_e = 25 \text{ W/m²K}\\)). In DRY Zurich, actual winter wind velocity is below **3 m/s** during **71%** of the heating period (Section 4.1, Table 3, p. 35–36). Adjusting wind velocity to 0 m/s improves calculated U-values by **2% to 20%** (average **8%** improvement for 4 uninsulated buildings), reducing calculated heating demand by **4% to 8%** (Section 4.1, p. 36).

### 3. Real Indoor Temperatures in Unheated Basements / Cellars
* **Source Title**: *The prebound-effect in detail: real indoor temperatures in basements and measured versus calculated U-values* (Hoffmann & Geissler, 2017)
* **Data Year**: Winter 2015/16 (12.10.2015 to 21.02.2016)
* **Measured Basement Air Temperatures**: Across 6 unheated basements with uninsulated cellar ceilings, measured indoor air temperatures ranged between a minimum of **13 °C** and a maximum of **21 °C** (at weekly outdoor mean temperatures between **-0.8 °C and 13.8 °C**) (Section 3.2, p. 34–35).
* **Measured vs. Calculated Basement Temperature Gap**: Measured basement temperatures were **around 8 K (mean 7.3 K)** higher than normative EN ISO 13789 / SIA 380/1 calculations (which yielded a calculated mean basement temperature of **8.3 °C** at an outdoor mean of **5.1 °C**, corresponding to a normative \\(b\\)-factor of **0.78**) (Section 2.2, p. 34, Section 3.2, p. 34 & Section 4.2, p. 36).
* **Temperature Spread Across Objects**: Excluding one building with a heat-emitting boiler room in the basement (Reinach 01), the temperature spread between the remaining 5 basements was only **0.5 to 2.5 K** (Section 3.2 & Section 4.2, p. 34–36).
* **Tested Building Cohort for Basement Temperatures** (Table 2, p. 34–35):
  * **Basel 07**: *Single-family house*, built 1928 (2/3 ground, 1/3 air)
  * **Münchenstein 01**: *Single-family house*, built 1925 (2/3 ground, 1/3 air)
  * **Basel 08**: *Multi-family house*, built ~1950 (ground)
  * **Basel 09**: *Multi-family house*, built ~1950 (2/3 ground, 1/3 air)
  * **Basel 10**: *Multi-family house*, built 1918 (2/3 ground, 1/3 air)
  * **Reinach 01**: *Multi-family house*, built 1962/63 (2/3 ground, 1/3 air; contains boiler room)
  * **Zürich Office**: *Office / Non-residential*, built 1951 (ground)
* **Impact on Calculated Heating Demand**: Using measured cellar temperatures (or an empirical \\(b\\)-factor of **0.5** instead of normative default **0.7–0.8**) reduces calculated heating demand by **5% to 10%** (Section 4.2 & Section 5, p. 36–37).

  * **Source Title**: *FAWA – Feldanalyse von Wärmepumpen-Anlagen* (BFE / FWS, 2004)

  * **Building Type**: Single-family and multi-family residential buildings (< 20 kWth capacity)
  * **Data Year**: 1994/95 to 2002
  * **Sample Size**: **N = 221 installations** with climate-normalized data (out of 236 total)
  * **Values & Findings**:
    * Ground-source (Sole/Wasser) heat pumps showed an average efficiency improvement of **1.2% per installation year** (Abbildung 37).

This was driven by heat pump technical improvements (+15%) and an increasing market share of ground-source systems (rising from 28% in 1995 to 43% in 2002).
